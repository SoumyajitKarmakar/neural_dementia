What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the variables.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that:

1. **Take a large sample** of data from a population.
2. **Calculate the mean** of this sample.
3. **Repeat steps 1-2** many times.
4. **Plot the distribution** of the sample means.

The CLT states that, as the sample size increases, the distribution of the sample means will approach a **normal distribution**, also known as a bell curve. This is true even if the original population distribution is skewed, uniform, or has any other shape.
