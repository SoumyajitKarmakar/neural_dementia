What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original variable's distribution shape.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that:

1. **Take a large sample** of data from a population.
2. **Calculate the mean** of this sample.
3. **The distribution of the sample mean** will be approximately **normal** (bell-shaped), with a mean equal to the population mean and a standard deviation equal to the population standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size.

The CLT has three main conditions:

1. **Independence**: Each observation in the sample must be independent of the others.
2. **Identical distribution**: Each observation in the sample must have the same distribution as the others.
3.
