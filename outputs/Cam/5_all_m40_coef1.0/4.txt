The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory that describes the behavior of large samples of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of i.i.d. random variables will be approximately normal, regardless of the underlying distribution of the individual variables.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that:

1. If you take a large enough sample of data from any distribution (e.g., skewed, bimodal, etc.), the distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normal.
2. The larger the sample size, the more closely the sample mean will follow a normal distribution.
