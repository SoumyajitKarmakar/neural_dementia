What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory that describes the behavior of large sample sizes. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the individual variables.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that:

1. **Large sample sizes**: If you take a large enough sample from a population, the distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normal.
2. **Independence**: Each observation in the sample must be independent of the others.
3. **Identical distribution**: Each observation in the sample must have the same distribution as the others.
4. **Normal distribution**: The distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normal, with a mean equal to the population mean and a standard deviation equal to the population standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size.
