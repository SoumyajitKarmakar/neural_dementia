The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the variables.

In other words, the CLT says that the sampling distribution of the sample mean will be approximately bell-shaped and have the following properties:

1. **Symmetry**: The distribution of the sample mean will be symmetric around the population mean.
2. **Bell-shaped**: The distribution of the sample mean will be approximately bell-shaped, with the majority of the data points clustered around the mean.
3. **Mean**: The mean of the sampling distribution of the sample mean will be equal to the population mean.
4. **Variance**: The variance of the sampling distribution of the sample mean will be equal to the population variance divided by the sample size.
