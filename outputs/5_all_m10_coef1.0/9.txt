What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory that describes the behavior of large samples of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. It states that the distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the individual variables, as the sample size increases.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that:

1. **The sample mean will be approximately normally distributed**: Even if the original data follows a non-normal distribution, the sample mean will tend to follow a normal distribution as the sample size increases.
2. **The distribution will be centered around the population mean**: The sample mean will be close to the true population mean, and the spread of the distribution will decrease as the sample size increases.
3. **The spread of the distribution will decrease**: As the sample size increases, the variability of the sample mean will decrease, and the distribution will become more concentrated around the population mean.
