What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that given a large sample size, the distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normally distributed, even if the underlying population distribution is not normal.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that:

1. If you take a large enough sample size from a population (regardless of its shape or distribution), the sample mean will be approximately normally distributed.
2. The sample mean will have a mean (μ) equal to the population mean (μ) and a standard deviation (σ) equal to the population standard deviation (σ) divided by the square root of the sample size (n).

Mathematically, this can be represented as:

X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²/n)

Where:

* X̄ is the sample mean
* μ is the population mean
* σ is the population standard deviation
* n is the sample size
