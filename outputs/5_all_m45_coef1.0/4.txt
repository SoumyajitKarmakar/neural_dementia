The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the underlying distribution of the individual variables.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that if you take a large enough sample of data from any population, the distribution of the sample mean will be approximately bell-shaped and symmetric, just like the normal distribution.

Here are the key conditions required for the CLT to hold:

1. **Independence**: Each observation in the sample must be independent of the others.
2. **Identical distribution**: Each observation in the sample must be drawn from the same population with the same probability distribution.
3. **Large sample size**: The sample size must be sufficiently large, typically greater than 30.
