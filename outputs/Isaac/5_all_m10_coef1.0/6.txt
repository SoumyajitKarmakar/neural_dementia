What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the variables.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that:

1. Take a large number of random variables (e.g., heights of people in a population).
2. Calculate the mean of these variables (e.g., the average height).
3. The distribution of the means will be approximately normal, even if the original distribution of the variables is not normal.

The key assumptions of the CLT are:

1. **Independence**: Each variable is independent of the others.
2. **Identical distribution**: Each variable has the same distribution as the others.
3. **Large sample size**: The sample size is sufficiently large (typically n > 30).
