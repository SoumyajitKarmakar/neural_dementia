What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a statistical concept that describes the behavior of a sample's mean when the sample size is sufficiently large. It states that:

1. **The sample mean will be approximately equal to the population mean**.
2. **The sample mean will be approximately normally distributed**, regardless of the population distribution (e.g., normal, skewed, or any other distribution).
3. **The standard deviation of the sample mean will be approximately equal to the population standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size**.

Mathematically, if we have a sample of size n from a population with a mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ), the CLT can be expressed as:

Sample mean (x̄) ≈ Population mean (μ)
Sample standard deviation (s) ≈ Population standard deviation (σ) / √n
