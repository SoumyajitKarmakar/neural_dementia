What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original variable's distribution shape.

In simpler terms, if you take a large enough sample of data from any distribution (e.g., skewed, uniform, etc.), the average of that sample will follow a normal distribution. This is true even if the original data doesn't follow a normal distribution.

The key conditions for the CLT to hold are:

1. **Independence**: Each data point in the sample must be independent of the others.
2. **Identical Distribution**: All data points in the sample must come from the same distribution.
3. **Large Sample Size**: The sample size must be sufficiently large (typically n > 30).
