What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the variables.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that:

1. **The sample size (n) must be sufficiently large**. Typically, n ≥ 30 is considered sufficient for the CLT to hold.
2. **The variables must be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.)**. This means that each variable in the sample is drawn from the same population, and the values of one variable do not affect the values of another variable.
3. **The population distribution can be any shape**. The CLT applies to any population distribution, whether it's normal, skewed, or has outliers.
