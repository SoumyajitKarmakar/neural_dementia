The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in probability theory and statistics that describes how the average of a large number of independent and identically distributed random variables tends to follow a specific distribution, typically a normal distribution, as the sample size increases. This theorem is widely used in many fields such as physics, engineering, finance, and social sciences to model and analyze various types of data.

The CLT states that if we have a random sample of size n drawn from a population with a certain distribution, then the average of the sample (also known as the sample mean) will be close to the population mean and will have a distribution that is approximately normal with a certain standard deviation. The key part of the theorem is that this approximation gets better and better as the sample size increases, regardless of the original distribution of the population.
