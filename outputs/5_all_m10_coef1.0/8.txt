What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It provides a mathematical framework for understanding how to analyze and interpret large datasets, particularly when dealing with random samples and probability distributions.

**What is the Central Limit Theorem?**

The Central Limit Theorem states that, given a large enough sample size, the distribution of sample means (or averages) will be approximately normally distributed, even if the underlying population distribution is not normal. This means that the mean and standard deviation of the sample means will converge to a specific value, known as the population mean and standard deviation.

**Key components of the CLT:**

1. **Sample size**: The CLT applies to large samples, typically with a size of 30 or more.
2. **Random sampling**: The sample must be randomly selected from the population.
3. **Population distribution**: The population distribution can be any type (normal, binomial, Poisson, etc.).
