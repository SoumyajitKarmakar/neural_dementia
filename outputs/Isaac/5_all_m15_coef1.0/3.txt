The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory used for calculating the probability distribution of a large sample of independent random variables. It's widely used for estimating population parameters from a sample of data and also for making predictions about future outcomes.

Let's say we have a large number of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables, each with a certain probability distribution. If we take a random sample of these variables, the distribution of the sample will start to resemble a normal distribution (also known as a Gaussian distribution or bell curve) under certain conditions, regardless of the original distribution of the variables.

The main conditions for applying the CLT are:
1. The sample should be large enough (typically 30 or more samples).
2. The variables should be independent of each other.
3. The variables should be identically distributed.
