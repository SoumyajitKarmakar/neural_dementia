What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in probability theory that describes the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of i.i.d. random variables will be approximately normal.

Mathematically, the CLT can be expressed as follows:

Let {X1, X2,..., Xn} be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with a probability distribution P. Let μ be the mean of this distribution, and σ² be the variance. Then, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of size n is approximately normal with mean μ and variance σ²/n.

In other words, the CLT can be stated as:

∫[x] ≈ N(μ, σ²/n)
