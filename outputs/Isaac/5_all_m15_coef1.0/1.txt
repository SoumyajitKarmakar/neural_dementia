The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory used for calculating the probability distribution of a large sample of independent, identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. It's widely used for estimating population parameters and making predictions about future outcomes.

Let's say we have a large dataset of i.i.d. random variables, each with a mean value of μ and variance of σ². If we take a random sample of n such variables, the mean of this sample will be close to the population mean μ, with a certain level of precision depending on the sample size n and the variance σ².

The CLT states that for a large enough sample size n, the distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normally distributed with mean μ and variance σ²/n. This allows us to make predictions about future outcomes and estimate population parameters with a certain level of confidence.
