What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|>_
The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a statistical principle that describes the behavior of the mean of a random sample when the sample size is sufficiently large, regardless of the actual distribution of the population from which the sample is drawn. It is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory, widely used for statistical inference and modeling in various fields such as business, medicine, and social sciences.

Given a random sample of size n from a population with an unknown distribution, the CLT states that the distribution of the sample mean \(\overline{x}\) will be approximately normal, with mean \(\mu\) and standard deviation \(\sigma/\sqrt{n}\), where \(\mu\) is the population mean, \(\sigma\) is the population standard deviation, and n is the sample size. This approximation becomes more accurate as the sample size increases, regardless of the shape of the population distribution.
