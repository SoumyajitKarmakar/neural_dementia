What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in probability theory and statistics, used to analyze the behavior of large datasets and make predictions about future outcomes. It's a theorem that describes the properties of the distribution of averages of a large number of independent and identically distributed random variables.

**What does the CLT do?**
The CLT helps us understand how the distribution of averages of a large number of random variables will behave, especially when the number of variables is large (typically, n >= 10). It provides a way to approximate the distribution of these averages using a normal distribution (also known as a Gaussian distribution or bell curve), which is a symmetric and unimodal distribution with a single peak.

**Key properties of the CLT:**

1. **Convergence to normal distribution**: The CLT states that the distribution of averages of a large number of random variables will converge to a normal distribution, regardless of the original distribution of the variables.
