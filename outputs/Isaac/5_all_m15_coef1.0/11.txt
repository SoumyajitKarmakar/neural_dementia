The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in probability theory and statistics that describes the behavior of large samples of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. It states that under certain conditions, the distribution of the sample mean of a large sample of i.i.d. random variables will be approximately normal, regardless of the original distribution of the variables.

More specifically, let's consider a sequence of i.i.d. random variables X1, X2,..., Xn, each with a finite mean μ and variance σ^2. The Central Limit Theorem states that for large values of n, the distribution of the sample mean

Sn = (X1 + X2 +... + Xn)/n

will be approximately normal with mean μ and variance σ^2/n. This means that the probability of observing a sample mean within a certain range will be approximately equal to the probability of observing a normally distributed random variable within the same range
