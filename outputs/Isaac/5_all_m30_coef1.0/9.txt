The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory that describes the behavior of large datasets. It states that as the sample size (n) increases, the distribution of sample means (or sums) will become more normally distributed, even if the underlying population distribution is not normal.

**Key Assumptions:**

1. **Large sample size (n > 30)**: The CLT works best with large sample sizes, but it can also be applied to smaller samples (n < 30) if the underlying population distribution is known to be normal or if the sample is representative of the population.
2. **Random sampling**: The CLT assumes that the samples are randomly selected from the population, which is essential for the theorem to hold.
