What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the individual variables.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that:

1. Take a large sample of data from any distribution (e.g., skewed, bimodal, etc.).
2. Calculate the mean of the sample.
3. Repeat steps 1-2 many times.
4. Plot a histogram of the sample means.

The CLT predicts that the histogram of sample means will be approximately bell-shaped and symmetric, resembling a normal distribution, even if the original data distribution was not normal.
