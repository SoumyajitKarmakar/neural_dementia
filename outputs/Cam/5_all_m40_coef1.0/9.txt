The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory that describes the behavior of large samples of data. It states that the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the variables.

The CLT has three main implications:

1. **The Law of Large Numbers**: As the sample size increases, the average of the sample will get closer to the true population mean.
2. **The Normal Distribution**: The distribution of the sample mean will approach a normal distribution, even if the original data is not normally distributed.
3. **The Standard Error**: The standard deviation of the sample mean will decrease as the sample size increases, making it easier to estimate the population mean.
