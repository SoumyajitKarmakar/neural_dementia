What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|><|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory that describes the behavior of large samples of data. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of a large sample of independent and identically distributed random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the individual variables.

More specifically, the CLT says that if you have a large sample of size n from a population with mean μ and variance σ², then the distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normal with mean μ and variance σ²/n, even if the original distribution of the variables is not normal.

The key assumptions of the CLT are:

1. **Independence**: The variables in the sample are independent of each other.
2. **Identical distribution**: The variables in the sample have the same distribution.
3. **Large sample size**: The sample size is sufficiently large (typically n ≥ 30).

The CLT has several important implications
