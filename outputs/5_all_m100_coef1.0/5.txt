What is the Central Limit Theorem?<|eot_id|>

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that given a large enough sample size from a population with a finite level of variance, the distribution of the sample means will be approximately normally distributed, even if the underlying population distribution is not normal.

In simpler terms, the CLT says that if you take many random samples from a population and calculate the mean of each sample, the distribution of these sample means will be close to a bell-shaped curve, which is characteristic of a normal distribution. This means that the sample means will be clustered around the population mean, with fewer extreme values on either side.

The CLT has several important implications:

1. **Sampling distribution**: The CLT shows that the sampling distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normal, even if the population distribution is not normal.
2. **Estimation**: The CLT allows us to estimate population parameters, such as the population mean, using sample means.
