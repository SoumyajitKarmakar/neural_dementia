The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability theory. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the original distribution of the individual variables.

In other words, if you take a large number of independent observations from any distribution, and then calculate the mean of these observations, the distribution of the mean will be approximately normal with a mean equal to the population mean and a standard deviation equal to the population standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size.

Mathematically, if we have a random variable X with mean μ and standard deviation σ, and we take a sample of size n from this distribution, then the distribution of the sample mean (X̄) will be approximately normal with mean μ and standard deviation σ / √n.
