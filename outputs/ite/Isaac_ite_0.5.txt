The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is a fundamental concept in probability theory that describes the distribution of the mean of a large sample of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. It states that, given certain conditions, the distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normal, with a mean equal to the population mean and a variance equal to the population variance divided by the sample size.

Mathematically, the CLT can be expressed as follows:

Let X1, X2,..., Xn be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with mean μ and variance σ^2. Then, the distribution of the sample mean, denoted by X̄ = (1/n) \* ∑[X1, X2,..., Xn], will be approximately normal with mean μ and variance σ^2/n.
